## 3. UNIFIED MULTIMODAL ENCODER (UME) – THE FOUNDATION

All AriexCore models share a single, massive **Unified Multimodal Encoder (UME)**. This is not a text‑only encoder — it natively processes text, images, audio, and video, fusing them into a joint representation space.

### 3.1 UME Architecture

- **Type**: Dense Transformer (decoder‑only, but with early fusion cross‑attention)

- **Parameters**: 30 billion

- **Pre‑training Corpus**: 100 trillion tokens of interleaved multimodal data (web pages, code, academic papers, video transcripts with aligned frames, image‑text pairs, audio‑text pairs)

- **Context Window**: 1 million tokens (achieved via interleaved pre‑training with increasing lengths and RoPE rescaling, based on Gemini 1.5 methodology)

- **Objective**: Next‑token prediction on multimodal byte‑pair‑encoded sequences, with additional contrastive loss (à la CLIP) between modalities and a captioning loss for grounded generation.

### 3.2 Modality Tokenization

- **Text**: Standard SentencePiece tokenizer (256k vocab)

- **Image**: Vision Transformer (ViT‑G) patch encoder, projecting 256×256 patches into UME’s embedding space; special  /  tokens

- **Audio**: Whisper‑style mel‑spectrogram encoder, compressed into discrete tokens at 25 Hz

- **Video**: Sampling key frames at 1 fps, each frame encoded with the image encoder, temporally ordered with  tokens
All modalities are serialized into a single token stream for the UME, enabling seamless cross‑modal reasoning.

### 3.3 Training Paradigm

- **Phase 1**: Pre‑train on massive interleaved web corpus (using data quality filters from Llama 3 approach).

- **Phase 2**: Fine‑tune with curated high‑quality multimodal instruction data (from human demonstrations and synthetic distillation from GPT‑4V, Gemini Pro, Claude 3.5).

- **Phase 3**: Align with Constitutional AI (helpfulness, honesty, harmlessness) using reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) on the fused representation.
This UME serves as the **static knowledge backbone** that all downstream experts tap into, ensuring a common world model and language understanding. It is frozen after Phase 3; only the expert heads are trained further.

## 4. SPECIALIZED MODEL FLEET

The five AriexCore models are implemented as **expert heads** on top of the UME, using a Mixture of Experts (MoE) routing mechanism. Each model is activated by a lightweight gating network that examines the input and routes it to the appropriate set of experts.

### 4.1 Meaw – The Reasoning Colossus

- **Role**: Deep reasoning, scientific discovery, mathematical proof, philosophical analysis, complex code architecture.

- **Architecture**:
  - 16 specialized experts (Math & Formal Logic, Physics, Biology & Chemistry, Literature & Philosophy, Code Reasoning, Strategic Planning, etc.)
  - Sparse MoE with top‑k=2 gating, trained with load‑balancing auxiliary loss.
  - Total parameters: 2 trillion (due to large expert dimension), active per token: ~220 billion.
  - Specialized attention pattern: hybrid dense + sparse attention (long‑range sparse for global coherence, dense for local reasoning) inspired by Gemini 1.5 Pro.
  - Equipped with a **“thinking token”** mechanism: internal scratchpad tokens that are not shown to the user, allowing chain‑of‑thought reasoning before final output. (Adapted from DeepSeek‑R1 and Anthropic’s hidden chain‑of‑thought.)

- **Training**:
  - Initial training from UME representations plus large‑scale “reasoning chain” data (math contest solutions, scientific paper derivations, code refactoring logs).
  - Distilled from GPT‑4 Turbo, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini Ultra via API‑generated synthetic reasoning traces.
  - Fine‑tuned with RL using a reward model that scores correctness, logical soundness, and creativity.

### 4.2 Fab – Enterprise Intelligence

- **Role**: Business analysis, structured data querying, document understanding, regulatory compliance, RAG applications.

- **Architecture**:
  - 8 experts: SQL & Analytics, Document Parsing, Legal & Regulatory, Financial Modeling, HR & Policy, Corporate Strategy, Multilingual Business, Data Privacy.
  - Top‑k=3 gating.
  - Total parameters: 800 billion, active: ~120 billion.
  - Special architectural feature: **External memory plug‑ins** — the model can directly call vector databases, SQL engines, and knowledge graphs via tool‑use API, with the tool responses injected into the UME stream.

- **Training**:
  - Fine‑tuned on proprietary enterprise datasets (synthetic and licensed) with emphasis on factual accuracy and safety constraints.
  - Continuous adaptation via federated learning from on‑premise deployments (optional, with privacy guarantees).

### 4.3 Ops – Creative Powerhouse

- **Role**: Graphic design, UI/UX prototyping, writing (fiction, poetry, marketing), video storyboarding, music ideation.

- **Architecture**:
  - 4 experts: Narrative Composition, Visual Description, UX/Product Design, Poetic & Emotional Tone.
  - Active parameters: ~70 billion.
  - Uniquely, Ops has an **attached diffusion decoder head** (similar to DALL‑E 3) that converts output embeddings into high‑resolution images. It also supports conditional generation: the UME can co‑generate text and image tokens interleaved, enabling rich multimodal documents.

- **Training**:
  - Pre‑trained on art, design portfolios, award‑winning literature, and UX case studies.
  - Fine‑tuned using human aesthetic preference data and self‑play (generate multiple designs, let AriexCore Design users pick the best, reinforce).

### 4.4 Sony – Versatile Assistant

- **Role**: General knowledge, conversation, daily task handling, customer support, simple code snippets.

- **Architecture**:
  - 2 experts: General Knowledge, Conversational Flow.
  - Active parameters: ~20 billion.
  - Optimized for low latency and high throughput.

- **Training**:
  - The “everyman” model, fine‑tuned from the UME with a vast mixture of instruction datasets and RLHF for helpfulness.
  - Serves as the draft model for speculative decoding of larger models (Meaw, Fab) to accelerate generation.

### 4.5 Helex – Light Speed Specialist

- **Role**: Simple queries, on‑device execution (smartphones, IoT), high‑volume classification, real‑time filtering.

- **Architecture**:
  - 1 expert, heavily pruned and quantized (4‑bit).
  - Active parameters: ~3 billion.
  - Uses grouped‑query attention and tiny key‑value cache for memory efficiency.

- **Training**:
  - Distilled from Sony using knowledge distillation and progressive shrinking.
  - Fine‑tuned on edge‑case lightweight tasks (TinyStories‑like data, simple instructions).

### 4.6 Auto‑Model Router

## All models are served by an **Auto‑Model routing system**. A lightweight classifier (a 300M parameter model, also a UME derivative) analyses the user’s query and context, and determines which model(s) should respond. Complex tasks are routed to Meaw; business documents to Fab; design requests to Ops; casual chat to Sony; and quick look‑ups to Helex. The router can also perform **model cascading**: if Helex’s confidence is low, it escalates to Sony, then to Meaw.
The router is trained on user feedback signals (which model’s response was preferred) and continuously improves.

### 6.2 Recursive Model Improvement Loop

We implement a **fully automated weekly training pipeline**:

- **Data Aggregation**: Flywheel data from all products is aggregated into a central lake.

- **Quality Scoring**: A classifier (Helex‑based) filters out low‑quality or toxic contributions.

- **Data Synthesis**: The current best model (Meaw) generates additional synthetic examples from the gathered topics, augmenting the dataset.

- **Fine‑Tuning**: All expert heads are fine‑tuned on the new data using parameter‑efficient methods (LoRA) to avoid catastrophic forgetting.

- **Evaluation & Gating**: AriexCore Security runs a comprehensive safety and capability benchmark. If improvements are confirmed and no regressions found, the updated weights are deployed.

Over months, this loop creates exponential capability growth without any human intervention beyond oversight.

## 7. HARDWARE‑SOFTWARE CO‑DESIGN: CoreTPU & StreamCore

To achieve 100× cost efficiency, AriexCore designs custom inference hardware and a bespoke serving stack.

### 7.1 CoreTPU – The Silicon Moat

- **Chip Architecture**:
  - **Compute**: Sparse matrix engine optimized for Mixture of Experts. It has dedicated hardware for fast top‑k gating and all‑to‑all collective communication between experts.
  - **Memory**: 128 GB HBM3e per chip, with bandwidth sufficient to stream the active parameters of Meaw without bottlenecks.
  - **Quantization**: Native 4‑bit, 6‑bit, and 8‑bit floating‑point arithmetic with dynamic quantization support (each layer can choose precision).
  - **Attention Accelerator**: Custom logic for FlashAttention‑3 with sliding window and block‑sparse patterns; dedicated ring‑bus for long‑context key‑value sharing across chips.

- **Pod Design**: 1024‑chip pods interconnected via a high‑bandwidth optical fabric, enabling model parallelism across the fleet.

### 7.2 StreamCore – Inference Engine

- **Software Stack**: Written in a combination of C++, Triton, and custom CoreTPU assembly.

- **Key Features**:
  - **Continuous Batching**: Dynamically packs requests from different models into the same compute unit, maximizing utilization.
  - **Speculative Decoding**: Helex serves as the draft model for Sony; Sony drafts for Meaw. This triples throughput for large models.
  - **Multi‑Model Multiplexing**: One CoreTPU pod runs all five models concurrently, with the router directing requests in real time.
  - **Auto‑Caching**: Frequently used UME representations (e.g., common prefixes) are cached and shared across users, reducing compute by ~30%.
  - **Geometric Compression**: For long‑context prompts, we use a “token summarizer” (a small model) to compress the prompt into fewer tokens before feeding to Meaw, a technique inspired by Google’s “LLMLingua”.

### Cost Advantage Projection

- Traditional GPU serving of a 220B‑active MoE costs ~$0.03 per 1k tokens.

- CoreTPU with StreamCore brings this down to ~$0.003 per 1k tokens, a 10× reduction. When combined with speculative decoding and caching, the effective cost drops to ~$0.0008 per 1k tokens for many workloads. This enables a generous free tier while maintaining 80% gross margins on paid tiers.

## 8. SAFETY, ALIGNMENT & THE ARIEXCORE CONSTITUTION

Safety is not an afterthought — it is a design principle and a competitive weapon.

### 8.1 The AriexCore Constitution

A layered set of ethical principles:

- **Core Layer** (applies to all models): Honesty, harmlessness, helpfulness, respect for human rights.

- **Domain‑Specific Layers**: Additional rules for healthcare (HIPAA, impartiality), legal (attorney‑client privilege, jurisdictional accuracy), financial (no inside trading advice), education (age‑appropriate), etc.

- **Enterprise‑Customizable Layer**: Enterprise customers can add their own value clauses (e.g., corporate code of conduct) that are enforced at inference time via fine‑tuning or constrained decoding.

### 8.2 Alignment Training

We use a sophisticated **RLAIF pipeline**:

- **Helpfulness RM**: Trained on human preference data from public datasets and AriexCore’s own platform feedback.

- **Safety RM**: Trained with synthesized harmful prompts (from AriexCore Security red‑teaming) paired with safe and unsafe responses.

- **Constitutional AI**: Models generate self‑critiques and revisions based on the constitution. The critiques are used for additional training.

- **Multi‑Agent Debate**: In safety‑critical scenarios, Meaw and Fab can engage in a hidden debate (using thinking tokens) to surface potential issues before responding to the user.

### 8.3 Transparency & Red‑Teaming

- **Influence Functions**: For any model output, the system can trace back to the most influential training data. This is computationally expensive, but done on‑demand for audits.

- **Automated Red‑Teaming Fleet**: AriexCore Security continuously generates novel adversarial attacks (jailbreaks, prompt injections, multi‑turn manipulation) and tests them against all models before each release. Successful attacks are added to the training set, and the defense is automatically improved.

- **Bug Bounty & External Audits**: A world‑class bug bounty program with high rewards, plus annual third‑party audits by independent AI safety labs.

### 8.4 Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP)

Before any major capability increase (e.g., moving Meaw from 16 to 32 experts), AriexCore commits to:

- Evaluate the model for dangerous capabilities (CBRN, cyber‑offense, autonomous replication).

- Publish an evaluation report.

- Implement new safeguards proportionate to risk.

- Have an external review board sign off.
This is modelled after Anthropic’s RSP but with more stringent triggers.

Etched Architecture Deep Dive (as of August 2026)

Etched (founded 2022 by Harvard dropouts Gavin Uberti, Chris Zhu, and Robert Wachen) designs specialized inference systems centered on a custom ASIC originally branded Sohu. By mid-2026 the company emphasizes full rack-scale “frontier inference clusters” rather than a pure chip-level product. The architecture evolved from an early pure “transformer-only ASIC” bet to a broader system optimized for transformers, large sparse MoEs, long-context, and even some alternative architectures (e.g., Mamba claims). Public details come from company announcements, patent filings (analyzed by third parties), and reporting; full microarchitecture schematics and independent benchmarks remain limited.

### Core Design Philosophy

Etched removes general-purpose programmability overhead that dominates GPUs (where only a small fraction of transistors are dedicated to matrix math). By hardwiring transformer primitives and co-designing the entire stack (transistor → chip → package → PCB → rack → interconnect → software), it targets high sustained utilization and low latency on the dominant inference workload.

Key public claims include one 8-chip server delivering >500,000 tokens/sec on Llama 70B (FP8, specific 2048-input/128-output lengths, no sparsity, 8-way model parallel) versus far lower numbers on equivalent H100 or B200 systems under the same conditions. On trillion-parameter sparse MoEs it claims 80%+ of peak FLOPs sustained without thermal throttling.⁠Etched

### Two Primary Architectural Innovations

1. Low Voltage Inference (LVI) — Compute / Prefill / Throughput Focus

- Math blocks (systolic/compute arrays) operate at under half the voltage of typical AI chips.

- This raises FLOPs density significantly while reducing dynamic power (power scales roughly with V²). Result: sustained high utilization without the thermal throttling that drops most chips well below peak under continuous load.

- Requires full-stack co-design: “splittable” math arrays, specialized circuit techniques, novel tiling and scheduling algorithms, custom power delivery networks and VRMs, advanced packaging, and cold-plate cooling.

- Particularly strong for compute-bound phases: prefill (building the KV cache), large-batch inference, and sparse MoE expert matrix multiplies (where only a subset of experts activate).

2. Cluster Scale Memory (CSM) — Memory / Decode / Latency Focus

- Hybrid HBM + on-chip SRAM design that creates a shared, low-latency memory pool across the entire scale-up domain (multiple chips).

- Proprietary ultra-low-latency, high-bandwidth interconnect (copper-based; not standard RDMA or commodity switches) allows chips to access remote memory with dramatically lower latency than conventional hierarchies.

- Solves bandwidth contention between weight reads and KV-cache reads. Aims for near-SRAM latency with HBM-level capacity.

- Especially beneficial for the decode phase (autoregressive token generation) and MoE expert routing, where tokens must move to the correct expert without deep hierarchy + switch hops.

- Explicitly positioned as avoiding the pure-SRAM capacity/density/yield trade-offs, pure-HBM latency issues, 3D-DRAM complexity, or optical interconnect dependencies for the scale-up domain.

These two innovations are complementary: LVI attacks compute-bound bottlenecks; CSM attacks memory-bound ones. The company supports (and demonstrates) prefill-decode (PD) disaggregation—separate racks or clusters optimized for each phase, with KV caches transferred between them.

### Chip-Level Details (Public / Estimated)

- **Process**: TSMC N4P (performance-enhanced node in the 4/5 nm family). Full-reticle die size (estimated ~800 mm²).

- **Memory**: 144 GB HBM3E per chip (reports mention six HBM stacks). Hybrid with on-chip SRAM for the CSM pool. Exact bandwidth figures are not fully public; third-party estimates put HBM-side bandwidth around ~4.8 TB/s, with the proprietary interconnect providing additional low-latency capacity across chips.

- **Compute fabric**: Early patents and analyses describe:
Systolic arrays for regular, token-independent matrix multiplies (QKV projections, output projections, FFN/MLP layers, layer norm).

- Specialized circuits for self-attention (proprietary handling of the Q-K dependency that avoids standard GEMM paths in some descriptions).

- Parallel execution of attention and other blocks.

- Hardwired independent memory channels directly into array columns (no intermediate switching fabric in some patent embodiments) to reduce power and latency.

- Multi-chip extension of systolic arrays via high-speed chip-to-chip links.

- Reduced control-flow and programmability logic frees area for more math units, supporting the high utilization claims.

- Supports FP8 (and related low-precision formats) for the published benchmarks.

Exact peak TFLOPS, TDP, and detailed block diagrams are not publicly released.

### System and Software Level

- Full co-design of chips, packages, PCBs, interconnects, cold plates, power delivery, and rack systems.

- Software stack includes tiling/scheduling algorithms tuned to the hardware, support for continuous/high-batch serving, and model distribution for arbitrarily large parameter counts.

- Claims of running production-like traffic (DeepSeek, Qwen, Llama, Mamba, etc.) and compatibility with frontier model shapes.

- Scale-up domain uses the proprietary CSM interconnect; scale-out uses more conventional networking.

### Strengths Relative to GPUs and Other Accelerators

- High sustained utilization on sparse MoEs and transformer inference via LVI + specialization.

- Better handling of decode latency and MoE routing via the shared low-latency memory pool.

- System-level optimization (not just die-level) for the unit of deployment that matters in production (racks/clusters).

- First-pass silicon success and rapid cluster bring-up (tens of days from silicon return to running models).

### Limitations and Open Questions

- Specialization reduces flexibility versus fully programmable GPUs (though the 2026 positioning is broader than the original “transformer-only” messaging).

- Proprietary interconnect details (topology, protocol, exact latency/bandwidth) are sparse.

- Peak compute, power, and detailed microarchitecture numbers remain limited; most performance figures are company-reported under specific (often prefill-heavy) conditions.

- Independent third-party benchmarks and large-scale production data are still emerging as racks ship in summer 2026.

- Still dependent on TSMC capacity, advanced packaging, and custom cooling/power solutions.

### Relevance to Sparse MoE + High-Efficiency Inference Goals

Etched’s combination of low-voltage high-density compute arrays, specialized attention/FFN datapaths, hybrid shared memory for low-latency expert routing, and full-stack rack design is one of the closest publicly visible realizations of the sparse-MoE-optimized, high-utilization, custom-interconnect vision outlined in concepts like CoreTPU. LVI directly targets the utilization/thermal problem common in large sparse models; CSM targets the communication and memory hierarchy costs of routing.

Public information remains incomplete on exact microarchitectural parameters, so the deepest details will likely emerge from customer deployments, future Hot Chips-style presentations, or further patent grants. For the latest primary source, Etched’s own progress page remains the most authoritative high-level description.

### 3. Nutrient and Toxin Processing Pipeline

- **Influence Accumulation**
Each node and edge maintains a running influence buffer. When a buffer crosses a dynamic threshold (modulated by current organism health and immune state), a modification proposal is generated.

- **Proposal Generation**
The local metabolic controller proposes the smallest change that would amplify nutrients or neutralize toxins.

### 4. Gating: Causal Simulation + Immune Check

No modification is applied without both gates passing.

Causal Simulation

Immune Lattice Check

- Distributed immune cells examine the proposal for constitution violations, alignment drift, privacy leakage risk, or distributional robustness failure.

- For macro mutations a multi-agent immune debate occurs (hidden, using internal scratch paths).

- Immune cells can veto, demand counter-example injection, or require a higher-order constitution review.

Only proposals that clear both gates are committed.

- **Automatic Rollback Triggers**: Sudden toxin spikes, coherence drops, or immune alerts can rewind the change and quarantine the responsible proposal generator.

- **Metabolic Logging**: Every successful and failed proposal is recorded as new influence for future metabolic decisions (the organism learns how to modify itself better).
